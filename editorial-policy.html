Editorial Policy

How we pick, test, and publish. Last updated October 2026.

How tools get selected for review

We review tools people are actually searching for. Selection starts with keyword research: if a meaningful number of people are searching for a tool by name, or searching for the problem it solves, it goes on our list.

We do not accept paid review requests. Tools cannot buy their way onto the site. No tool has ever been added to the review queue because someone asked us to cover it.

How we test

Every review starts with a real account signup using personal details, not a press account. We run the same standard task battery for each tool category: the same prompts, the same workflows, the same edge cases.

Tools are scored on five criteria: Output Quality, Ease of Use, Value for Money, Feature Depth, and Free Tier Usefulness. Each criterion is rated 1 to 5. The overall score is a weighted average.

Affiliate relationships

Some links on this site are affiliate links. If you click one and make a purchase, we may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. We use this to keep the site running and funded.

Affiliate relationships do not affect scores or rankings. Scores are set during testing, before any affiliate deal is in place. A tool can have an affiliate program and still score a 3.0 if that is what it earns.

No tool has ever paid to be ranked higher, featured more prominently, or reviewed more favorably. Commission rates are not a factor in any editorial decision.

No advertiser influence

No tool or company has ever paid to appear higher in a ranking, receive a better score, or be featured on this site. We do not accept sponsored placements, paid inclusions, or brand deals that influence editorial content.

If a company reaches out to dispute a score, we re-test if they provide evidence of a meaningful change. We do not adjust scores in response to complaints alone.

Update policy

Scores are not static. We update a review when any of the following happens:

  • The tool changes its pricing in a way that materially affects value.
  • A major feature update changes what the tool can do.
  • New competitors enter the market and shift what a good score means at that price point.

Every review shows the date it was last updated. If a review is more than 12 months old without an update, treat the score as a starting point and check recent user feedback before purchasing.
