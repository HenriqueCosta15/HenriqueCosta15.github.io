Quick Answer

Choose Cursor if: you want the most polished AI IDE experience, the best tab-chaining autocomplete, and you don't mind paying $20/month for a tool that has had more time to mature.

Choose Windsurf if: you want to save $10/month, you want a more generous free tier, or you want to use Cascade to delegate longer autonomous tasks to the AI rather than directing every edit manually.

The Core Difference: Manual vs Agentic

Cursor and Windsurf are built on the same VS Code foundation, but they have different philosophies about how the AI should work.

Cursor is built around the assumption that you want to stay in control. Cmd+K lets you edit a selection with a natural language instruction. Cmd+L opens a context-aware chat. Tab chains autocomplete suggestions so you can move through repetitive edits quickly. The AI assists, you direct.

Windsurf bets more on Cascade, its agentic AI mode. Cascade doesn't just respond to your instructions: it plans. Give it a feature to build and it will figure out which files to touch, write the code, run the terminal commands to test it, observe the output, and fix anything that went wrong, all without you intervening. The goal is to hand it a task and come back when it's done.

Both approaches work. Which one fits you depends on how much you want to supervise the AI versus how much you want to delegate to it.

Autocomplete Quality

Cursor has an edge here. Cursor Tab is the best autocomplete experience in any AI IDE right now. It predicts not just your next characters but your next edit, and chains suggestions so you can Tab through a repetitive change in seconds. Renaming a variable, updating a list of similar calls, filling out a pattern: Cursor Tab handles these with an almost eerie accuracy.

Windsurf's Supercomplete is excellent too. Codeium had years of autocomplete-specific work before Windsurf existed, and it shows in the suggestion quality. But Windsurf doesn't have Cursor's predictive edit chaining yet, which makes repetitive refactors slightly more manual.

Agentic Coding: Cascade vs Composer

Windsurf wins this round. Cascade is meaningfully better than Cursor's Composer at autonomous multi-step tasks. You can tell Cascade "add rate limiting to all the API endpoints" and it will plan the approach, touch the right files, install any needed dependencies, test the result, and iterate if something breaks. It's not perfect and it occasionally needs correction, but the ambition and reliability are ahead of where Cursor's equivalent is right now.

Cursor's Composer feature is improving and is capable, but it requires more steering. You're still directing steps more than delegating goals. For developers who want to hand off a task and review the diff rather than co-author every line, Windsurf is currently the better tool.

Pricing: $10 vs $20

This is where Windsurf has a clear structural advantage. Windsurf Pro is $10/month. Cursor Pro is $20/month. Both are paid per user with no usage caps on AI requests at the Pro level.

Over a year that's $120 extra for Cursor. For a solo developer that's a real difference. For a team of five that's $600/year. The gap is hard to justify unless Cursor's polish genuinely saves you enough time to offset the cost, and for most developers it probably doesn't.

The free tier difference also matters for developers who don't code full-time. Windsurf's free tier includes unlimited autocomplete plus a daily allowance of flows. Cursor's free tier caps completions at 2,000/month, which can run out quickly on active projects.

Which Should You Pick?

For professional developers doing daily feature work who want the most refined experience and the best autocomplete chaining: Cursor is still slightly ahead on polish. The tab predictions and multi-file editing feel more reliable for complex, precise tasks.

For most developers: especially those who want to try an AI IDE, who code part-time, or who want to delegate longer tasks to an agent: Windsurf is the better deal. Half the price, a more generous free tier, and Cascade is genuinely impressive for the tasks it handles well.

The real answer: both have free tiers. Spend a week with each. The one you naturally reach for is the right one for how you work.

Verdict: Tie on Quality, Windsurf Wins on Value

Both tools are excellent. Cursor has marginally better autocomplete chaining and more mature multi-file editing. Windsurf has Cascade for autonomous tasks, a more generous free plan, and a Pro tier that costs half as much. Unless you specifically need Cursor's Tab chaining on daily repetitive edits, Windsurf is the smarter financial choice for most developers.
