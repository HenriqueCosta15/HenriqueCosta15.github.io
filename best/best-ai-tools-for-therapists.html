How Therapists Use AI Appropriately

The boundary for appropriate AI use in therapy practice is clear: administrative and business tasks yes, patient information no. Here is where each category of tool fits:

Documentation templates: ChatGPT is excellent for building note templates, treatment plan frameworks, and intake paperwork structures using de-identified or generic examples. Build the template once, adapt it to each client in your own system. The efficiency gain is significant without any privacy exposure.

Practice administration: Notion AI organizes the business side of your practice. Supervision notes, CPD logs, policy documents, vendor contacts, and professional reading all belong here. Grammarly and QuillBot handle the written communication that goes out: referral letters, insurance correspondence, and professional emails.

Marketing and client attraction: Canva and Rytr cover visual and written marketing content. Website copy, social media posts, blog articles on mental health topics, and psychoeducation handouts. Consistent online presence improves referral quality and reduces the feast-or-famine cycle common in private practice.

Client resources: ElevenLabs enables audio versions of psychoeducation content and guided exercises. Clients engage more consistently with audio than written take-home materials in most settings.

Privacy and Ethics: What Therapists Must Know Before Using AI

The ethical and legal boundaries around AI use in therapy practice are clear and non-negotiable:

Never input PHI without a BAA: Patient-identifiable information (names, dates, diagnoses, session content) must never enter a standard AI tool. Most consumer AI tools including ChatGPT, QuillBot, and Grammarly are not HIPAA-covered by default. Using them with patient data is a potential HIPAA violation regardless of your intent.

Check your licensing board: Standards for AI use in mental health practice are evolving rapidly. Your licensing board and professional association may have issued guidance since this was written. Consult those resources before integrating AI into any clinical workflow.

Maintain clinical judgment: AI cannot substitute for clinical assessment, formulation, or treatment decisions. Any AI-assisted content shared with clients should be reviewed for accuracy and appropriateness before distribution. AI does not know your client.

Supervision applies: The same professional accountability that governs your clinical work applies to AI-assisted work. If you would discuss a clinical decision in supervision, discuss the AI-assisted version too.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it ethical for therapists to use AI?

Using AI for administrative tasks, practice marketing, professional writing, and documentation templates is generally appropriate and consistent with good practice. The ethical lines are clear: do not enter patient-identifiable information into AI tools without a HIPAA-compliant Business Associate Agreement, do not use AI to generate clinical assessments or treatment decisions, and ensure any AI-assisted content you share with clients is reviewed and accurate. Consult your licensing board and professional association guidelines, as standards are evolving.

Can AI help therapists with note writing?

AI can help create note templates, session note frameworks, and documentation structures using de-identified examples. Do not enter actual patient information into standard AI tools. Some HIPAA-covered platforms offer AI-assisted note features within a compliant environment. The efficiency gain from good note templates is real, but the privacy boundary is non-negotiable.

What is the best AI tool for a therapist in private practice?

ChatGPT for all writing tasks that do not involve patient information: intake forms, practice policies, psychoeducation handouts, and professional communication. Canva for visual practice materials and social media. Notion for organizing the business side of your practice. The combination of these three covers most of what a solo private practice needs from AI.
