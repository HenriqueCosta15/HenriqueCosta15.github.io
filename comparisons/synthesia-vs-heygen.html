Quick Answer

Choose Synthesia if: you're a business team producing corporate training, multilingual content, or product explainers at scale. The 140+ language support, 160+ avatar library, and enterprise reliability justify the higher price for teams where video quality and consistency directly affect outcomes.

Choose HeyGen if: you're a solo creator, small team, or anyone building a faceless YouTube channel who wants strong avatar video quality without paying enterprise prices. HeyGen has closed the quality gap and costs significantly less for individuals.

Avatar Quality: How Close Is the Gap?

Synthesia held a clear lead on avatar realism for years. In 2026 that gap has narrowed considerably. HeyGen's avatars look natural, lip sync accurately, and hold up well in most business and content creator contexts. Side by side, Synthesia's avatars still have a slight edge on fine facial expressions and movement naturalness, but the difference is no longer dramatic.

For most use cases, both tools produce videos where viewers accept the avatar as a presenter without significant distraction. Where Synthesia still wins: close-up shots, longer videos where subtle movements accumulate, and situations where maximum realism is critical (executive communications, high-stakes client-facing content).

Language Support: Synthesia's Biggest Advantage

This is where Synthesia pulls away decisively. 140+ languages with accurate lip sync is a category-defining feature. If you need to produce the same video in English, Spanish, Japanese, Arabic, and Portuguese, Synthesia does it from a single script with the same avatar speaking each language naturally. HeyGen supports 40+ languages, which covers most Western markets, but falls short for truly global operations.

For businesses serving international audiences or running multilingual campaigns, this alone justifies Synthesia's premium. For creators targeting English-speaking audiences primarily, it's largely irrelevant.

Pricing: Where HeyGen Wins Clearly

At the entry level, both start at $29/month. But Synthesia's $29 Starter plan caps you at 10 videos per month, while HeyGen's Creator plan at the same price gives you unlimited videos. To get unlimited on Synthesia, you're paying $89/month. That's a $720/year difference for the same output volume.

Custom avatar creation follows the same pattern. Synthesia charges $89/month (Creator) for it. HeyGen includes it at $29/month. For solo creators building a personal brand or faceless channel, HeyGen's pricing structure is hard to argue with.

Ease of Use and Editor

Both tools are beginner-friendly. Neither requires technical knowledge or video production experience. Synthesia's editor is slightly more polished, with better template variety and a cleaner slide-building workflow. HeyGen's editor is more minimal but gets the job done for most use cases. If you need to produce structured, multi-slide training videos with branded templates, Synthesia's editor is noticeably better. If you're making single-scene talking-head videos for YouTube or social, the difference is marginal.

Speed

HeyGen generates videos faster, typically in 1-2 minutes versus Synthesia's 3-5 minutes. For high-volume production, that adds up. For most users producing a handful of videos per week, it's a minor difference.

Verdict: Synthesia for Business Teams, HeyGen for Creators

If you're running a business that needs multilingual video content at scale, or a team producing corporate training and explainer videos where reliability and language depth matter, Synthesia is worth the premium. The 140+ language support and enterprise feature set have no real equivalent.

If you're a solo creator, YouTuber, or small team who wants professional AI avatar videos without spending $89/month, HeyGen is the smarter pick. Avatar quality is strong, pricing is genuinely fair, and custom avatars are accessible at the entry tier. Start with HeyGen's free plan to test quality, and only upgrade to Synthesia if you hit the language or enterprise limits.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Synthesia or HeyGen better?

Synthesia is better for enterprise teams who need 140+ language support and production at scale. HeyGen is better for solo creators and small teams who want strong avatar quality at a lower price. Both are capable tools, and the right choice depends almost entirely on your language needs and budget.

What is the difference between Synthesia and HeyGen?

Synthesia has more languages (140+ vs 40+), a larger pre-built avatar library (160+ vs 100+), and stronger enterprise features like SSO and brand kits. HeyGen is cheaper, generates videos faster, and includes unlimited videos and custom avatars at a much lower price point, making it better value for individuals.

Which is cheaper, Synthesia or HeyGen?

HeyGen is significantly cheaper for unlimited video production. Both start at $29/month, but Synthesia's $29 plan limits you to 10 videos. Unlimited videos on Synthesia costs $89/month. HeyGen includes unlimited videos on its $29 Creator plan, a $720/year saving for equivalent output.

Can I create a custom avatar on both?

Yes. Synthesia requires a 5-minute video and includes custom avatar creation on the Creator plan ($89/month). HeyGen also offers custom avatar creation, but includes it at a lower price tier. Both produce high-quality results, though Synthesia's custom avatars have a slight edge on natural movement.

Which is better for YouTube?

For YouTube, HeyGen is usually the better choice. The pricing is more sustainable for content creators producing regular videos, the avatar quality is strong enough for most YouTube audiences, and the faster generation speed suits a higher-volume content schedule. Synthesia's language depth is overkill for most English-language YouTube channels.
