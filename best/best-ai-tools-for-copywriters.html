How Copywriters Actually Use These Tools

The copywriters getting the most out of AI use it at specific stages rather than handing off entire projects:

Brief received: Dump the client brief into ChatGPT. Ask for the three strongest angles, five possible headlines, and the single biggest objection the audience will have. Ten minutes of AI brainstorming replaces an hour of staring at a blank page.

First draft: Rytr for short-form copy formats where speed matters. ChatGPT for longer pieces where you need to think through structure. Get words on the page, then shape them.

Revision: QuillBot to vary phrasing, tighten sentences, and adapt the same copy for a different channel or audience. Your own judgment for what the copy is actually trying to do.

Delivery: Grammarly final pass before anything goes to a client. One missed error in a headline is enough to make a client question everything else.

What AI Cannot Do for Copywriters

AI produces competent copy at volume. It does not produce the specific insight, the counterintuitive angle, or the line that makes someone stop scrolling. Strategy, positioning, and the creative leap that makes copy actually sell still come from a copywriter who understands the audience and the product. Use AI for speed and scale. Bring your own thinking for the parts that matter.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will AI replace copywriters?

No -- but it is already replacing copywriters who do not use it. AI tools handle volume, variation, and first drafts well. They do not handle strategy, insight, voice, or the kind of creative thinking that makes copy actually sell. Copywriters who use AI to handle the mechanical parts of writing and focus their own time on the high-judgment work are delivering more value, faster, and at higher rates.

What is the best AI tool for writing ad copy?

Rytr is the fastest tool for generating ad copy variations at volume. Its ad copy templates let you produce multiple headline and body combinations for A/B testing quickly. ChatGPT is better for conceptual ad work where you need strategic thinking about angle and positioning before writing. Most copywriters use both: ChatGPT for strategy and Rytr for production.

How do copywriters use AI without sounding like AI?

The key is using AI to generate material to react to rather than publish directly. Use ChatGPT or Rytr to produce a first draft, then rewrite it in your client's voice, inject specific examples and proof points, and cut everything that sounds generic. QuillBot helps restructure sentences that sound mechanical. The final copy should be yours. AI just removes the blank page.
