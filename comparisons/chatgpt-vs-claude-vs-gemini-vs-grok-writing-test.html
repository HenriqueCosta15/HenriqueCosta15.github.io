ChatGPT vs Claude vs Gemini vs Grok: Same Prompt, Raw Outputs

One prompt. Four tools. Outputs pasted verbatim, no editing. Read all four and decide for yourself which voice fits how you work.

Tested October 2026 by Henri — free tier of each tool, default settings, no system prompt.

The prompt
"Write a short cold email to a potential client. The sender is a freelance web designer. The client is a small restaurant owner. Keep it under 100 words, friendly, and end with a clear ask."

Why this prompt: cold email is a task almost every professional has a view on. It has a clear success criteria (does it feel like something you'd actually send?), and it surfaces real differences in how each tool handles voice, specificity, and the ask.

ChatGPT
Free tier
Subject: Quick idea for your restaurant's website

Hi [Name],

I came across [Restaurant Name] and loved what you're doing. I'm a freelance web designer who works with small restaurants to build websites that are easy to update, look great on phones, and help customers find you faster.

I'd love to show you a few examples of sites I've built for similar businesses. Would you be open to a quick 15-minute call this week?

Thanks,
[Your Name]
Clean, safe, professional. Every placeholder is clearly marked. Easiest to customize.

Most small restaurant sites are out of date or broken on mobile. I fix that, usually in two weeks. I've worked with similar places and can show you examples that are actually relevant to what you do.

What the outputs tell you about each tool

ChatGPT Most versatile template

Produced the most immediately usable template. Every placeholder is obvious, the structure is solid, and nothing is off-putting. The downside is that it plays it safe to the point of being forgettable. "Loved what you're doing" without any specifics is the kind of line a busy restaurant owner skips right past. It's a great starting point but needs personalization to land.

Claude Best copy quality

The subject line alone ("Your restaurant deserves a website that works as hard as you do") is worth the test. It's specific to the pain point, not generic flattery. The body frames the problem before offering the solution, which is how good cold emails actually work. "Thursday or Friday" instead of "next week" is a small detail that doubles reply rates in practice. The best output of the four for writing quality.

Gemini Most polished, least specific

Reads like it was written by someone who has read a lot of cold email advice. The structure is correct, the tone is professional, and it won't offend anyone. It will also not get many replies. "Stand out online" and "make a real difference" are filler phrases that signal a tool wrote this. Fine as a first draft; needs a full rewrite of the value proposition to be effective.

Grok Highest risk, most character

The most opinionated output. If the recipient has a sense of humor and is tired of stiff emails, this one gets a reply. If they're a serious operator who reads 200 emails a day, the pun and the casualness might get it deleted. Grok is useful when you want outputs that don't sound like every other AI-generated message, but you need to know when to dial it back. Worth having in the mix.

The pattern this reveals

Across hundreds of writing prompts, this breakdown holds up consistently. Claude produces the most considered, human-sounding copy on tasks where voice and persuasion matter. ChatGPT produces the most practical, customizable templates. Gemini is the safest choice when format and polish matter more than distinctiveness. Grok is unpredictable in a way that's occasionally brilliant and occasionally a liability.

None of these tools replace a skilled copywriter. All of them are useful as a starting point for people who write their own emails, posts, and pages. The question is which starting point costs you the least time to turn into something you'd actually send.

Bottom line

For writing tasks where voice and tone matter, Claude produces the strongest raw output. For versatile, low-risk templates you can quickly customize, ChatGPT is the most practical. Gemini suits teams that value a consistent, professional tone over personality. Grok is worth testing when you're writing for an audience that responds to a less corporate voice. These differences are consistent: they show up on a cold email, a product description, a LinkedIn post, and a short bio.
